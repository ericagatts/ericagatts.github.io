Genomic Testing Portal

Reinventing the genomic test ordering experience for oncologists — moving from fragmented, paper-heavy workflows to a modern, patient-centric platform built on Salesforce.

Foundation Medicine
Principal Designer
2025
Salesforce / Web

Background

Genomic testing at the intersection of oncology and data

Foundation Medicine provides physicians a suite of genomic profiling tests and clinical reporting services designed to identify a cancer patient's biomarkers and unique genetic signatures. Genomic testing supports oncologists' clinical decision making — providing them critical information to select treatment pathways, precision oncology therapies, and clinical trials most appropriate for their patients' individual needs.

The end-to-end genomic testing journey, from diagnosis through clinical reporting.

A patient newly diagnosed with cancer meets with their oncologist, who recommends comprehensive genomic testing. The oncologist orders the appropriate Foundation Medicine tests — either online or via paper form. The specimen is processed, sequenced, and analyzed, and a detailed genomic report is delivered back to the oncologist to inform the next step of their patient's cancer care.

"The design strategy was focused on elevating Foundation Medicine's genomic testing experience to support a robust, patient-centric experience for oncologists — simplifying complexity to deliver meaningful experiences for the people who need them most."

The Project

Project Phoenix — reimagining the ordering experience

As part of an organization-wide mandate to migrate Foundation Medicine's products, data, and front-end systems to the Salesforce platform, the Product Design team was tasked with redesigning the comprehensive genomic test ordering portal and test results platform from the ground up.

The design strategy was focused on elevating Foundation Medicine's genomic testing experience to support a robust, patient-centric experience for oncologists — simplifying complex clinical workflows while delivering measurable improvements in order completion rates and clinician satisfaction.

Discover

Understanding Our Users' Needs

1 / 4
Front Desk

Ethnographic research conducted with existing portal users revealed four distinct ordering archetypes, each with a different relationship to the portal. Front office staff are primarily responsible for initiating final orders, handling billing issues, and retrieving reports for oncologists. Nurses and residents "own" the clinical data entry side of ordering and coordinate closely with oncologists for orders on hold and sharing reports for consultation. Oncologists are the primary consumers of the genomic reports and are the clinical decision-makers—though they rarely initiate orders themselves. Pathologists play a critical upstream role in specimen coordination and finalizing order—and in some settings, act as the ordering physician entirely. Designing for this ecosystem meant accounting for handoffs, shared workflows, and role-specific information needs across every touchpoint.

Mapping the Current State

Heuristic Evaluation
📐

Design Standards

Much of the current portal does not conform to current design standards and users' expectations, creating an inconsistent and confusing user experience.

💡

Hidden Value

The portal does not promote its own value to users. Customers may not know of all the features available to them beyond just ordering a FMI test.

🧭

Navigation & Wayfinding

Navigation and wayfinding in the portal is confusing—and sometimes non-existent—leading users to dead-ends and unexpected spots.

🔐

Login Friction

Login and forgot password flows are clumsy and frustrating to oncologists who "do not want to remember yet another password."

👤

Lack of Personalization

The portal is not customized to users' individual needs, missing notification preferences, team management, order favorites, and frequently used features.

📋

Ordering Pain Points

The ordering process is fraught with pain points, including its overwhelming length, the need to fetch additional paperwork, and an ever-expanding list of tests.

🤝

Collaborative Gaps

Ordering FMI's tests is collaborative, often requiring multiple users to fill out different portions of the form—but the portal does not support these flows.

🔬

Test Selection

There is little explanation of what test would be most appropriate for the patient before the user begins filling out the order form.

❓

No Help System

There is no help functionality built into the portal, leaving users to either trouble-shoot on their own, or call FMI for answers.

Reflections

Lessons learned

Forging partnerships across the organization proved to be the single most critical factor for success. Complex enterprise design doesn't succeed in isolation — it requires deep collaboration across clinical, engineering, and business teams from day one.

Collaborative development of requirements, scope, and MVP ensured alignment and prevented costly rework downstream. Knowing which pieces could be compromised versus negotiated — and communicating that clearly — kept the team moving. Above all: prioritize, reprioritize, and prioritize again.
